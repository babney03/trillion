The Northwest year

What's happening in your yard right now

A wet, mild climate has its own calendar. Here's what we're doing each stretch of the year, and what it's for.

March – May

Waking it up, and getting ahead of the moss

Growth goes vertical almost overnight here. Everything this season is about getting the lawn dense enough to crowd weeds out before summer, and clearing the winter's moss while it's still soft.

  • Moss treatment, then rake and overseed the bare patches
  • Core aeration and lime to fight our acidic soil
  • Bed cleanup, fresh edges and the first mulch round
  • Weekly mowing resumes, cut high to shade out weeds
June – August

The dry stretch nobody plans for

Our summers are drier than people expect. Lawns go dormant, new plantings die fast, and irrigation problems only show up when it's already too late. This is a watching season more than a cutting one.

  • Mow height raised — shorter grass burns first
  • Irrigation checks: heads, coverage, controller schedule
  • Summer pruning on shrubs that flowered in spring
  • Best window for patio and wall builds — dry ground
September – November

The busiest eight weeks of the year

Leaves and rain arrive together. Everything now is about moving water and organic matter off the property before it mats down, holds moisture against the lawn, and starts next spring's moss.

  • Repeat leaf passes — not one big cleanup in December
  • Gutters and downspouts cleared before the first real storm
  • Best planting window of the year — roots set over winter
  • Drainage fixes while we can see exactly where water sits
December – February

Quiet above ground, busy below it

Mild and wet rather than frozen, so the work never fully stops. This is when structure gets fixed and the next year gets planned, at lower rates than peak season.

  • Biweekly visits — growth slows but debris doesn't
  • Dormant pruning on fruit trees and deciduous structure
  • Storm response — windthrow and blocked drains
  • Design work drawn now, built first thing in spring

Before you call

Straight answers

What does monthly grounds care actually cost?
Most Eastside properties run $180–$340 a month, averaged across the year so you pay the same in February as in June. The number depends on lot size, bed area and how much tree cover drops on you in the fall. We price it at the walkthrough, in writing.
Do you charge for estimates?
No. Walkthroughs are free for both grounds care and project work. For design-build over about $15,000 we charge a design fee, credited in full against the build if you go ahead.
Is it the same crew every time?
Yes, and that's the whole point of a set route. You'll meet your crew lead at the walkthrough. If they're out, we tell you before the visit rather than sending strangers into your back garden.
Can you fix a yard that floods every winter?
Usually. Most Eastside flooding is downspouts dumping at the foundation and compacted clay with nowhere to drain. We'll come out during a wet stretch — seeing where water actually sits beats guessing in August.
Do you take one-off jobs, or contracts only?
Both. Seasonal cleanups, a single pruning day and project work are all standalone. Recurring care is a rolling agreement you can end with thirty days' notice — no annual lock-in.
