The Island year

What your garden needs, and when

Wet winters, a genuinely dry summer, and a narrow window for the work that matters. Here's how the year runs.

March – May

Turn the water back on before you need it

Everything wakes at once here. The jobs this season are about getting irrigation tested while a fault is still cheap to fix, and getting beds under control before growth outruns you.

  • Irrigation start-up: charge the system, check every head
  • Bed clean-up, fresh edges and the first mulch
  • Moss treatment, seeding and lawn repair
  • Planting, while the ground still holds moisture
June – August

The dry stretch people underestimate

Island summers get genuinely dry, and watering restrictions arrive right when plants need it most. A properly zoned system uses less water than a hose and keeps new planting alive.

  • Controller scheduling around watering restrictions
  • Drip conversion for beds and hedges — less waste
  • Hedge trimming and shaping
  • Best window for patios and walls — dry ground
September – November

Blow the lines out before the first freeze

The busiest weeks of our year. Water left in a line splits it, and you won't find out until spring. Fall is also the best planting window on the coast — roots establish all winter.

  • Irrigation blow-outs and winterizing — book early
  • Best planting window of the year
  • Leaf clearing and seasonal tidy-ups
  • Drainage fixes while you can see where water sits
December – February

Quiet season, and the right time to plan

Mild and wet rather than frozen, so structural work carries on. Design drawn now gets built first thing in spring, ahead of the queue.

  • Design and planning for spring builds
  • Dormant pruning and tree work
  • Storm damage and blocked drains
  • Hardscape work — ground handles it fine

Before you call

Straight answers

Is there a minimum project size?
Yes — $2,000 for landscaping projects. It means we can schedule properly and price fairly for the work we do take on. Irrigation repairs and smaller garden care jobs are handled separately, so ask.
Do you charge for quotes?
Not inside Nanaimo, for landscaping or complete irrigation installations. More than fifteen minutes outside our base near North Jinglepot there's a $100 fee, and for Ladysmith, Qualicum, Parksville or Gabriola it's worth asking before you book.
Do I really need irrigation on the Island?
Our summers are drier than the reputation suggests, and watering restrictions land exactly when new planting is most vulnerable. A zoned system uses less water than hand-watering and it runs whether or not you're home. It's the difference between replacing plants and keeping them.
When should I book a fall blow-out?
Early. Water left sitting in a line splits it over winter and you won't know until spring, when the repair costs more than the blow-out would have. Fall is our busiest stretch, so the calendar fills.
Do you maintain gardens you didn't build?
Yes. Garden and bed maintenance, hedge trimming, tree work and weed control, whether or not the garden started with us.
