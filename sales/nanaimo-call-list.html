Prospecting · Nanaimo BC

Nanaimo call list

Six businesses with real reviews and a findable weakness, pulled from public listings. Ranked by how sellable they look, not by how good they are.

The thing they all have in common — lead with this.

Every single business below has conflicting opening hours across its listings. COCO Café's own site says open seven days; Yelp says closed Mondays and Tuesdays. Shear Agony's hours differ between two directories, and so does its street address. Bodhi's Saturday hours vary by hours depending on which site you read.

That is not a cosmetic problem. A customer who drives to a bakery on a day a listing said it was open does not come back, and the owner never finds out why. It costs them money quietly, which is exactly the kind of thing an owner will sit still for ninety seconds to hear about.

It is also the thing you already solve better than anyone pitching them: a live open/closed panel reading from one schedule, plus correct opening-hours markup feeding Google. You have built it twice. It demos in five seconds on a phone.

Their web identity is scattered. Listings give two different domains for them — beeskneescafe.ca and beeskneesnanaimo.ca — ordering is pushed to a third-party site, and a similarly named Vancouver business is muddying their search results. Before you contact anyone

Ten minutes that stops you looking foolish

  1. Open their website on your phone. I could not — my connection blocks outbound traffic, so every judgement above about their site is inference from listings, not a review. This is the one step you cannot skip.
  2. Check their Google listing against their own site. Where the hours disagree, screenshot both. That screenshot is your entire pitch and it takes thirty seconds.
  3. Find the owner's name. About page, Facebook, or the Google listing. "Hi there" halves your reply rate.
  4. Confirm the numbers. Review counts above come from Tripadvisor, Yelp, Fresha and aggregator sites — different platforms, not comparable, and some are stale. Quote Google's figure, not mine.
  5. Build the site before you write. The whole approach depends on having something finished to hand over.
What I could not verify

I have not seen any of these websites. Outbound traffic from my environment is blocked — I tested a known-good address as a control and it failed identically — so everything here comes from search listings and directory data.

That cuts both ways. The hours and addresses I'm citing as inconsistent are themselves from those directories, so some may simply be out of date rather than wrong on the business's end. Verify before you put any of it in an email. Being corrected on a fact in your opening line costs you the meeting.

Six names, four of them worth a real attempt. Work top-down, three messages per business, then stop.
